How to read the map

Brisbane's bus route numbers aren't random. Routes are grouped by their first two digits, and each group mostly serves one part of the city: 38x is The Gap, 44x is Moggill, 22x is Wynnum. Bus RADS shows the area each group serves, the routes themselves and every stop they use, one depot at a time or across the whole city.

Quick start

  1. 1
    Pick a view. Choose Toowong, Carina or All at the top of the panel. All shows every Brisbane City Council bus route.
  2. 2
    Click a group in the list, such as 38x, to zoom to it and fade the others. Click it again to zoom back out.
  3. 3
    Hover or click a route number such as 411 to see its path and only the stops it serves.
  4. 4
    Zoom in and point at any stop to see which routes call there and how often.

The panel

Depot

Toowong and Carina show the routes on each depot's list. All shows every Brisbane City Council bus route, about 220 of them in 40-odd groups. The choice is saved in the web address, so a link ending in #carina or #all opens straight to that view. The browser's Back button steps between views.

On a phone

  • The panel becomes a sheet along the bottom. Tap the little handle at its top to shrink it and see more map.
  • There's no hovering on a touch screen, so tap a stop. Its pop-up lists the routes as buttons; tap one to highlight that route.
  • Tapping a family or hub heading folds or opens it.
  • The site follows your phone's light or dark setting.

How zones are worked out

Everything is calculated from Translink's timetable for one typical weekday, shown at the bottom of the panel.

  1. Groups. A route belongs to the group of its first two digits: 380, 381 and N385 are all 38x. Routes numbered under 100 (29, 61, 77…) are each a group of their own.
  2. Who owns a stop. For every stop, count the weekday trips from each group. If one group runs 60% or more of them, the stop is that group's. Otherwise it's shared and shown grey.
  3. City and hubs left out. Stops within 1.7 km of the Queen Street Mall, and hub stops such as UQ, are left out of the zones. Every group passes through them, so they'd only blur where the routes really start.
  4. From stops to areas. Each stop gets the area that's closer to it than to any other stop, but no more than 400 m away, about a five-minute walk. A group's zone is all of its stops' areas joined up.
  5. Main paths and variants. A path used by at least 15% of a route's trips in one direction is a main path; anything less is a variant.

Which group owns a stop depends on which routes are counted, so a stop shared in the All view can belong to one group in a depot view, and the other way round.

What it doesn't show

  • School (7xx) and special (9xx) runs. They aren't in Translink's main timetable feed. Note that the 7xx numbers in the feed are Gold Coast routes.
  • Weekends and nights. Trip counts are for one weekday. NightLink routes (N…) appear in their group but mostly don't run on a weekday.
  • Which depot runs each trip. The timetable doesn't say. Depot views come from each depot's route list, and a route worked by two depots appears in both.
  • The road map is a sketch. Roads, rail and the river are traced from the bus, train and ferry paths in the timetable, not from a street map.
